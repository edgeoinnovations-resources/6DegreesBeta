It started at dinner

I was planning to host some of my Dubai friends, including Dave and Melissa, for our weekly dinner when I learned that Dee, my friend from Doha who currently lives in Addis Ababa, was in town for a conference. Of course I invited Dee and her friend Sarah to join us, knowing that international teachers are always cool to hang out with other international teachers, even if they’d never met before.

If you’re an international educator, you know what happened next. We started playing the “Oh, you worked in X? You must know Y! We used to work together in Z!” game. After more connections than I can count, someone eventually mentioned Kevin Bacon. And then one of us — I’m not really sure who — said, “We should build an app!” And we all agreed.

The thing is, none of us at that dinner knows how to build an app or a site. But I knew that our Dubai friend and colleague Paul had the right combination of curiosity and skill to make it happen. When we proposed the idea to him the next day, he didn’t hesitate to jump in.

That was over a year ago. In the meantime we’d have spurts of WhatsApp chats and an occasional Zoom meeting, trying to figure out what we really wanted this site to be. And in the background, Paul was upskilling and tinkering and taking it all in. And then one day he dropped the beta on us. We entered our data and commented and questioned and Paul responded, and now we feel like it’s ready to release to the world of international education. We’re so excited to share it with you!

— Linda

What this is, and what it isn’t

This site is about connection

  • A way to re-connect with old friends and colleagues.
  • A way to visualise your connections with other international school teachers.
  • A way to find shared connections.

This site is not

  • A place to provide opinions about schools and colleagues.
  • A place to promote yourself and your career.
  • A place that will sell your data.

How the six degrees work

Every connection here is worked out from two things only: where you have been and when. Nobody rates anybody, and nobody decides who you know.

  • 1 — same school, same time
  • 2 — same school, different time
  • 3 — same city, same time
  • 4 — same city, different time
  • 5 — same country, same time
  • 6 — same country, different time

You can also confirm that you actually know someone — socially, professionally, or as family. Both of you have to agree before it shows, either of you can undo it, and it never changes your degree. Place and time decide that, and only place and time.

What we decided before we built it

In June 2026 the six of us sat down for ninety minutes to answer the questions that get harder the longer you defer them. This is what came out of it.

Things that are off the table, permanently

  • Selling your data.
  • Selling email addresses.
  • Letting outside tools scrape the connections.
  • Advertising built on tracking what you do here.

There is a real difference between making money from the data and making money from the service that holds it. One of those we might one day do to cover our costs. The other ends the project. As we put it at the time: one breach of trust ends this. The international community is too small for second chances.

What we ask you for

  • Your name, and the name you actually go by if it’s different.
  • Where you have worked, and when. Including schools back home — the whole point is who knows who.
  • Your email, used to sign you in and nothing else.

That is the entire list. We dropped nationality and subject area in September 2026 because they fed nothing. Nobody ever sees your email address — not other members, and not us.

Leaving

You can change anything you entered, at any time, and your connections recalculate. Leaving is different, and we want to be straight about why. Your history is part of other people’s connections too — if you and I taught together, that is a fact about both of us. So when you leave, your name comes off and your profile becomes a ghost: unnamed, unsearchable, and still holding the shape of the connections other people made with you. We would rather tell you that now than bury it in terms nobody reads.

Who can join

Invitation only, by someone already here. That was Dave’s idea from the very first week, and it is the main thing keeping this a community rather than a directory.

Who we are

Six international school teachers and one dinner party: Paul, Linda, Dee, Sarah, Dave and Melissa. Nobody is paid. Nobody has sold anything. It exists because a room full of teachers could not stop finding people they had in common.

6 Degrees is in beta and run by teachers in their spare time. If something is wrong — a school in the wrong city, a connection that makes no sense — tell whoever invited you, and it gets fixed.
