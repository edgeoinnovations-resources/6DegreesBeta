Before you start: two ideas the whole app rests on

Read this page once — every feature uses these two words.

1 · Relationship degree (1–6)

How strong a single connection between two people is, based purely on whether they shared a school / city / country, and whether they were there at the same time. It is pre-computed for every pair — the app never guesses it.

1 · same school, same time 2 · same school, different time 3 · same city, same time 4 · same city, different time 5 · same country, same time 6 · same country, different time

Colour runs on one scale: degree 1 is the most saturated turquoise, degree 6 the faintest. The same six colours are used in every view.

2 · Separation (hop count)

The classic "six degrees of separation" — the fewest steps to get from one person to another by hopping along connections. A direct link is 1 hop; a friend-of-a-friend is 2. The app computes this live with a breadth-first search.

Don't mix them up. "Degree" describes one link's strength (1–6). "Separation" counts how many links you cross between two people. In this demo community the average separation is about 1.6 hops and the widest gap (the diameter) is 3 hops.

The colour scheme

Turquoise #17A2B8 is the primary colour. Orange #FF6B35 is reserved for two things only: the person you're focused on, and a mutually verified link (it earns a badge).

Getting around

  • The eight features are tabs down the left side. Your focused teacher shows in the top-right and carries between views.
  • Most views have a search-as-you-type box — start typing a name, specialization, or nationality.
  • Default focus is Sarah Mitchell (T001), the demo "hero" who has one clean example of every degree 1–6.

Ego graph

"Who is this one person connected to, and how closely?"

One teacher sits at the centre. Everyone they're connected to is placed on a ring — the closer the ring, the stronger the connection (ring 1 = same school same time, out to ring 6). Bigger dots = people with more connections overall.

How to use it

  • Re-centre on anyone by clicking their dot, or by typing a name in the Center on box.
  • Hover a dot to see why the link exists — the shared school/city/country and the years that overlapped.
  • An orange ring + badge means the connection is mutually verified (both people confirmed it).
Demo tip: Start on Sarah Mitchell. She has exactly one clean example on every ring — James Thompson on ring 1 (same school, overlapping years) all the way out to Hannah Eriksson on ring 6 (same country, different years). It's the cleanest way to teach the 1–6 idea.

2

Force-directed network

"Show me the whole community at once."

Every teacher is a dot; every connection is a line, coloured by its degree. Related people naturally cluster together. This is the big-picture view — spread it out, filter it down, and view it flat or in 3D.

Spread it out

The Spread slider pushes the dots further apart so you can actually see who links to whom. Slide it up if the web looks too clustered, down to pull it back together.

2D or 3D

  • 2D (flat): drag a dot to pull the web around, scroll to zoom, click a dot for that person's postings.
  • 3D (spin & fly): the cluster floats in space — drag to spin it around, scroll to fly in and out, right-drag to pan, and click a dot to fly the camera right up to that person. The Auto-orbit button slowly circles the whole cluster for a hands-free fly-around.

Filter & style (works in both)

  • Degree checkboxes (1–6) turn each class of line on or off — uncheck 4, 5, 6 to leave only the strong same-school and same-city links and watch the clusters tighten.
  • Node size: by number of connections, or by years of experience.
  • Colour by: strongest link's degree, or home region (Middle East, Asia, Europe…).
Demo tip: Switch to 3D, nudge Spread up a couple of notches, then press Auto-orbit — the community turns slowly in space while you talk. Click any dot to fly straight to that person.

Adjacency matrix

"Which schools share the most people?"

A grid of schools against schools. Each square is shaded by how many teachers worked at both of those schools — darker turquoise means more shared people. The diagonal is each school's own headcount.

How to use it

  • Hover a square to see the count and the names of the shared teachers.
  • Order rows/columns by: country cluster (dense blocks appear along the diagonal — schools in the same country trade staff), most-shared first, or alphabetical.

Why a grid instead of more dots? At full scale the network view becomes a hairball. A matrix stays readable no matter how many schools you add — dense regions show up as solid blocks.

Demo tip: Order by country cluster and point at the square blocks hugging the diagonal — that's a regional teacher market visible at a glance.

Chord diagram

"Where do teachers go next?"

A circle of places. Each ribbon is a flow of teachers moving from one place to another, read from people's consecutive postings. The thicker the ribbon, the more teachers made that move; the arrow shows direction.

How to use it

  • Flows between toggle: country ↔ country (the big migration picture) or school ↔ school (the busiest individual schools only, for readability).
  • Hover a ribbon for the exact "from → to" and the number of moves; hover an arc for a place's total moves in and out.
Demo tip: Stay at country level and find the thickest ribbon — that's the strongest "career corridor" in the community (e.g. teachers moving between Gulf states). It answers a recruiter's first question: where does talent flow?

Map (2D · 3D · globe)

"Show the community on the actual world."

A real world map with every school as a circle — bigger circles are schools more community members passed through. Coloured arcs show people moving between schools.

How to use it

  • Click a school to open its roster — who taught there and in which years, as a mini timeline.
  • Migration arcs (toggle): each arc is a teacher move from one school to another; thicker arcs = more moves. Click an arc for the count.
  • 3D headcount columns (toggle): tilts the map and raises a column over each city, taller where more members worked.
  • Globe projection (toggle): wraps the map onto a globe for the "worldwide community" look.
  • Fly the journey: pick a teacher and press play — the camera hops through their postings in order, popping up each school.
Demo tip: Pick Sarah Mitchell and Fly the journey — the camera travels Bangkok → Dubai → Singapore, turning her CV into a story you can watch.
Uses a free, no-key basemap, so it always loads. Arcs and 3D columns are drawn with deck.gl; if a network blocks it, the school circles still work on their own.

6

Timeline (Gantt)

"Watch a connection actually form."

Time runs left to right. Each teacher gets a row, and each posting is a coloured bar spanning the years they were there. This is where you see a relationship degree being created.

How to use it

  • Compare A vs B: pick two people. Wherever their bars cover the same school, city, or country at the same time, that span is highlighted in orange and labelled with the degree it creates.
  • Colour by: school or region.
  • Year slider: drag the vertical "now" line across the years to scrub through the community's history.
Demo tip: The default pair — Sarah Mitchell vs James Thompson — overlaps at the American School of Dubai from 2017–2019. That highlighted orange band is their degree-1 connection, shown forming in real time.

8

Search & pathfinder

"Find people, and trace how any two are linked."

Five tools share this tab; pick one from the row of buttons at the top.

Faceted search

Filter teachers by country, city, school, role, subject, minimum years, curriculum, or verified-only. Each result links straight into the ego graph.

Pathfinder

Pick person A and person B and see the chain that links them, each hop labelled with its degree and the shared context. Three modes:

  • Featured demo chain — the curated warm-intro story (all strong, degree-1 hops).
  • Strongest links — favours the strongest connections even if it takes an extra hop.
  • Fewest hops — the shortest possible chain, the literal degrees of separation.
Why three modes? The "best" path depends on what you mean by best. The demo's signature chain Sarah → Grace → Carlos → Imani is three warm, same-school hops — great for a quality-of-introduction story. But the graph also has a shorter two-hop route between the same pair. "Fewest hops" shows that one. Both are true; they answer different questions.

Who do I know at School X?

Pick a school and a maximum link strength — get the people you're already connected to there, ready for a warm intro.

Reverse recruiting

Build a query like "people within 2 degrees of Sarah who taught Physics in 2+ countries" and get a shortlist table.

Natural language (coming soon)

A preview of asking questions in plain English. It's a deliberate stub — the real version will run safely on the server, never in your browser.

Running & editing it

For the non-developer keeping this alive.

See it live

It's already online at edgeoinnovations-resources.github.io/6DegreesBeta/. Nothing to install to view it.

Run it on your own computer

Because it loads a data file, it needs a tiny local server (double-clicking the HTML won't work):

  • Open Terminal in the project folder.
  • Run python3 -m http.server 8000
  • Open http://localhost:8000/ in a browser.

Change the data

All the people, schools, postings, and connections live in one file: data/demo_data.json. Edit it (or regenerate it from the CSVs) and every view updates — no rebuilding. When you later move to a live database, only one small file, js/loadData.js, changes; nothing else moves.

Privacy by design: salary and supervisor fields are kept blank and are never shown anywhere. No passwords or keys live in the site's files.
